**Where and who.** The AI assistant has three pages in **Project settings › Extensions › TestPulse Studio**, under **AI assistant**: **Connection**, **Functions and data**, **Log and failures**. Reserved for project administrators: anyone else sees no field at all — only why. Every change is attributed and dated. *Connection* and *Functions and data* are saved like every page of the administration: a draft, then **Save** in the banner at the top. Members use the assistant through the AI functions that are turned on.

**Connection — the state.** A banner says whether the connection is set up, and with which access mode. **Test the connection** makes a minimal call to the provider and shows either success (model and duration) or the provider’s technical detail, with any key removed; **the test uses the draft** — save to keep it. This detail is shown **only to administrators**: a member who meets an AI error reads a neutral sentence, never an endpoint, an identifier or the provider’s answer. When the provider does not answer at all, the page lists the three possible causes, in order: host unreachable or misspelt, **CORS** refused for the extension’s origin (given), network or corporate proxy. The administration may call any HTTPS address, because the endpoint is yours to enter; the other pages of TestPulse Studio may not.

**Connection — three access modes**, as three cards, from the most to the least watertight:
- **Entra ID** — each member signs in with their own identity, and nothing secret is stored. **Not available in this version**: the card is shown, but cannot be selected.
- **Enterprise gateway** — TestPulse Studio calls your gateway (APIM, proxy), which exposes the chosen provider’s API and holds its key. No key goes through the browser. The gateway must allow, by **CORS**, the origin of the extension’s pages.
- **Direct API key** — the key is typed once and **never shown again**, not even to an administrator. It is **masked on screen, not confidential**: the key is masked on screen but remains accessible to any project member with developer tools, since it passes through members’ browsers when a function calls the model. Use a key dedicated to TestPulse Studio, with a limited quota, that you can revoke.

**Connection — provider and address.** The proposed models depend on the provider. A provider **outside your Azure tenant** (OpenAI, Mistral, Gemini, Anthropic) shows a warning as soon as it is chosen — the data checked in *Functions and data* will leave your tenant — and saving asks for an explicit confirmation; changing provider asks again. **Anthropic is reachable only through an enterprise gateway.** Then the gateway address or the endpoint, starting with `https://`.

**Azure AI Foundry — the API format, not the maker.** A Foundry resource serves models of several families, each through the API it exposes: **Chat Completions (OpenAI v1)** for OpenAI, Mistral and other families, **Messages (Anthropic)** for Claude models. The **API format** is chosen for the default model, the review model and each model overridden in *Functions and data* (inherited from the default model until changed); existing connections stay on Chat Completions. Studio never guesses the format from the deployment name: when **Test the connection** gets a “404” (“api_not_supported”) on Chat Completions, the page offers “This deployment looks served by the Messages API (Anthropic) — try this format?”, and one click sets the format of the tested model and runs the test again. Paste the address of the resource, or the one Foundry shows (`…/openai/v1`, `…/anthropic/v1/messages`, on `…openai.azure.com` as on `…services.ai.azure.com`): Studio keeps the host and adds the route of the chosen format; an address that points to the other format is flagged under the field. In the Messages format, the key goes in `x-api-key`; Entra ID and the enterprise gateway work as with Chat Completions. A parameter the model refuses (the temperature, for example) is removed and the call goes on; reasoning effort and verbosity are not sent in this format.

**Connection — the key.** Once saved, the page says « Key saved, masked », who saved it and when. **Replace…** opens a new entry field; the current key stays in place until the new one is saved. **Revoke…** asks for confirmation, then removes the key: AI functions stop for every member of the project until a new key is saved. Revoke it at the provider too.

**Connection — models.** The **default model**, then the **judge model**, used by the judge, on demand, for *Designed-case review*: same provider, same key — no second secret. It has its own **Test this model** button. Pick a different model from the one that designs: when both are the same, the page warns that the review loses its value. Empty, the review is unavailable. The **answer limit** goes from 1,000 to 16,000 tokens; below 12,000, a warning says that the assessment of a long requirement may be cut. A switch says that the model **reasons** before answering: Studio then sends from the start the parameters a reasoning model accepts, without the two refusals (≈ 0.5 s each) an undeclared model pays on its first call. It applies to the default model; the judge model has its own, and so does each model overridden in *Functions and data* (until it is set, the box is inherited from the default model). When **Test the connection** or **Test this model** finds that a model refuses `max_tokens` or `temperature`, the page offers “This model looks like a reasoning model — declare it?”: one click ticks the box of the tested model, in the draft. Without a declaration, Studio keeps the adaptation for the next calls of the page. **Verified capabilities**: **Test images** sends a tiny generated image to the test design model and records whether it accepted it; a good result on one model says nothing about another.

**Functions and data — the nine functions**, in two groups. *Before the plan — the requirement* (six): *Requirement assessment*, *Requirement improvement*, *Test design*, *Designed-case review*, *Comparison with the code*, *Automated tests*. *During the campaign — the plan* (three): *Campaign analysis*, *Coverage axes*, *Test classification*. Each row shows whether it is **On**, the function, its **Model** — empty uses the default model of the connection; the judge always uses the judge model — and where that model comes from. **Reasoning effort** (Model default, Low, Medium, High, Very high) and **Verbosity** (Model default, Low, Medium, High) are set per function — for *Designed-case review*, they are those of the judge model. “Model default” sends nothing. A lower effort shortens the model’s thinking, hence the duration and cost of the call, at the risk of a less thorough answer; a low verbosity shortens the answer. A model that refuses the setting ignores it: the call succeeds, and **Latest failures** says so (“Setting not supported by the model: ignored”). The values advised per function are still to be validated on the bench. Anthropic and Gemini do not receive these settings yet: the page says so. Every function is **off when shipped**. This is the project’s switch: a configuration profile can then turn a campaign function off for its plans (*Configuration profiles › AI functions*), never back on.

**Functions and data — data sent to the model.** Seven boxes decide what may enter a prompt, whatever the function: test case titles and steps; requirement title, description and criteria; error messages and run comments; images attached to requirements; work items linked to the requirement; Azure DevOps image attachments; source code excerpts. Then **Replace people’s names with aliases**. An unchecked box never enters a prompt.

**Log and failures** (read-only). The **change log** of the connection keeps its last 50 changes, signed and dated: **sorted by date** (newest first, reversible), filters by category with their counts — *Connection*, *Models*, *Functions*, *Data*, *Key*, and *Grid* for the migrations of the test grid —, a search that ignores accents and case, each change as before → after, and **Export to CSV**. It never holds a key. The other pages have no detailed log: their last change is dated at the top of the page. **Latest failures** lists the last 10 failures of the AI functions, across all members: date, function, model (or « model not reached »), a readable cause with its code, and **Show the detail**. It also lists the **retries that succeeded** (“Retried, then succeeded”, with what was wrong in the first answer) and the **answers kept with gaps marked** (“Kept, gaps marked to complete”, with their paths). A transient provider error (500, 502, 503, 504, 408, network down) is **retried automatically**, at most twice, after 1 then 3 seconds; an exceeded quota (429) that announces a wait of 60 seconds or less is waited for and retried once — during the wait, the line of the running operation shows “Provider quota reached — retrying in N s”, and **Cancel** stops it; beyond 60 seconds, the error states the announced wait. Members see nothing when the retry succeeds; the list counts it (“Retried, then succeeded”, with the original status and the number of retries), and a failure after retries says so in its detail. A timeout and a cancellation are never retried. Members never see the detail: their message names the cause only (the project’s AI connection refused by the provider, access refused, quota reached, gateway refusing, connection missing or incomplete); the text sent and the model’s answer are never kept.

**Key refused (401).** When the latest failure is the provider refusing the project’s key (invalid, expired or revoked) after it was saved, the key row of **Connection** says **Key refused by the provider (401)** with the date of that failure and offers **Replace the key**: AI functions fail for every member until it is replaced.
