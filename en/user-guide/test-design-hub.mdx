**Where.** The **Design** area of the TestPulse Studio group, between Coverage and Tools, with one sub-tab, **Test design**. A link can open it directly on a requirement (`exigence=` in the address); from an uncovered rule, the link also carries the rule (`covers=RB-04`), which is highlighted in the test model and pre-selected when you add a case by hand.

**The steps.** The screen uses the full width and follows **steps**: *1 Test model* · *2 Techniques and plan* · *3 Cases* · *4 Review and write*, each with its state (“18 rules · 21 inputs · 8 points to clarify”, “42 planned cases”, “40 generated of 42 planned”, “40 to write”); a step that is not possible yet is greyed and says why on hover and focus. On the right, a **summary** always in view (below 1,280 px, it sits above the step, folded to one line — total, rules, the step's action — and *Show the summary* unfolds it): the number of planned cases and its split by technique (colour and name), the rules covered “N / M” with the **clickable list of those not covered** (a click opens the Rules tab, filtered, on the rule), the points to clarify, the step's action (*Continue: techniques and plan →*, then *Generate the N cases*, then *Review and write →*) and **What is sent to the model**, which unfolds the context settings (step 4 below); the bar per technique, the uncovered rules, the matrix and the budget are under *Plan detail*, tokens and durations under *Technical details*. Everything the summary shows is computed, never asked of the AI.

**One action at a time.** Each screen shows one main action — the only filled button — and it follows the state: *Analyse the requirement*, then *Continue*; *Generate the N cases*, then *Review and write →* (*Regenerate* becomes secondary); *Write N cases* in the bar at the top of step 4. An action that cannot run yet is shown as a secondary button, with the reason on hover. Details are folded, never removed: one click unfolds them, and the fold is remembered for you. The banner shows the current step with a filled circle and an underline; once a requirement is chosen, the selector shrinks to one line (*Change story* opens it again).

**1 · Choose a requirement** — by id, area path, iteration or query, as in *Coverage*. Only User Stories, Product Backlog Items, Requirements and Features are accepted; another work item is refused with a sentence. At the top, the **level and type** resolved from the Design section of the profile, with where they come from (« Level: System testing · Type: Functional testing · from the profile “Web e-commerce” »); **change for this generation** lets you pick another level or type for this generation only — never saved outside the profile.

**2 · See what already covers it — without AI.** The **Existing coverage** panel lists, for each acceptance criterion, whether it is *Covered*, *Partial* or *Not covered*, with the linked test cases next to it; for each ISTQB technique, how many tests already carry it (a former tag `technique:nominal` / `technique:negative` counts as equivalence partitioning, marked « former tag »); and **by rule**, the rules the linked tests declare they cover (`covers:` tags). The panel folds away; folded, its chips recall the requirement, its criteria and its linked tests.

**3 · The test model — the AI reads, you correct.** **Analyse** sends the requirement (and the context you checked, step 4) to the AI. The requirement is sent **as written**: special characters (`<`, `>`, `&`…), lines, table cells and Gherkin examples are kept, and so are the spaces that matter; when a field goes over the sending limit (40,000 characters), a banner says so above the model (“Text cut before sending: description…”) — never a silent cut. The AI returns a **test model**: the **rules** of the requirement — its own ids `RB-xx` kept as they are, otherwise numbered `R-1, R-2…`, each tied to the excerpt it comes from —, the **attributes** — what the tester sets or the starting state (an input, a choice, a precondition); an observable result (displayed, saved, refused, a message) is never one — (ordered, list, text or yes/no) with their **partitions**, valid and invalid, each with **one concrete representative value** or, for a field left empty, the **empty value**, shown “(empty)”, the **combined conditions** (a decision table) and the **states and transitions** when the requirement describes a lifecycle. A gap local to one input or partition no longer makes the whole analysis fail: a representative value that only repeats the name of the partition is emptied and the partition is **to complete** (the model counts it and *Generate* waits for its value); unreadable bounds, bounds on a non-ordered input, an unknown rule or dependency are removed. Only a gap of the whole model (a rule of the requirement missing, a duplicate id, an inconsistent condition) is sent back once to the AI assistant, then fails. When the requirement numbers its rules, an `R-n` rule is added only for a testable constraint no numbered rule covers (required, length, format…); the flow, ergonomics and non-functional requirements do not create any. What the requirement does not decide becomes a **point to clarify**, shown under the rules with its excerpt and **Copy** (one line per point, for the Product Owner; in Azure DevOps, where the page cannot write to the clipboard, *Copy* opens the text already selected: Ctrl+C (⌘C) is enough) — nothing is ever generated for it. Concurrency, atomicity between sessions, load or performance are not tested by hand: they become a point to clarify, linked to their rule, never a condition. A model that does not hold up (a partition without a value that is not declared empty, a value repeating its partition's label, limits on a value that is not ordered or that are neither numbers nor dates, an unknown rule…) is refused and asked again once; if it still fails, a sentence says so and nothing is shown half-way. The model reads in **tabs**, with their counts: **Rules** (the requirement's numbered rules, then the `R-n` constraints, each with its coverage — “✓ 3 cases”, “✕ no case”, “? point to clarify” —, its excerpt on demand, a “Not covered” or “Changed” filter and a search), **Inputs and partitions** (a table grouped by role — preconditions and starting state, form inputs, user choices —, validity as an icon and a word, the value in a fixed font with visible spaces “␣”), **Conditions** (one readable decision table per condition, reference column highlighted, reduced or full; its **nature** — *Validation* when each column accepts or refuses depending on its values, country × postal code, or *Behaviour* when each column has its own effect, the default address —, given by the AI for every condition — a model from an earlier analysis without it shows « Nature to choose » and is planned as *Behaviour* until you choose — and changed in one click; and its **infeasible combinations**, values that cannot be true together with their reason — no address yet and an existing default address —, proposed by the AI when the requirement makes them obvious, added or removed by you: an infeasible column stays visible, greyed and struck through, and is never planned) and **Points to clarify**. The tabs work with the keyboard (arrows). On a very large screen (2,400 px and more), Rules and Inputs show side by side: a click on a rule filters the inputs that cite it, and the other way round. The model is **yours**: add, rename or delete a rule, an input, a partition, a value — a partition is edited on its row (*Save* / *Cancel*, Enter / Escape), an input's role in its editing. **Exclude** keeps a rule visible, struck through, out of the coverage and the plan; *Restore* brings it back. **Analyse again** reads the requirement again **starting from the current model, your corrections included**: ids are kept, only what the requirement contradicts or what is missing changes, and the screen says what changed (« 1 added, 0 removed, 2 changed »); a failed analysis leaves the model as it was. Without AI, you can build the model by hand. An incomplete model (a partition without a value or a rule) is flagged, never completed for you.

**4 · Choose what is sent — document by document.** Nothing is checked by default. *Linked work items*, *Attachments* (DOCX and PDF **not supported in v1**), *Images* (PNG, JPEG, WebP, **reduced** to 1,024 px at most when you check them, only when the administration's image test has validated the model), *Pasted excerpt*. A category unchecked in the project's *AI assistant › Functions and data* shows **turned off for the project** instead of its boxes. The block says **what is sent at each step**: for the analysis, the requirement and the context checked; for the generation, the corrected test model and the selected coverage elements with their data — never a quantity instruction. The **estimated budget** is shown in parts (analysis, generation) and recalculated at every check; above 8,000 tokens a warning says it will be slower and more expensive — it never blocks.

**5 · Applicable techniques — deduced by the code, never by the AI.** At step 2, **one card per technique** of the ISTQB syllabus v4.0, with its reason and the number of cases it plans, which unfolds (the equivalence cases, the boundary values per input and partition) — the format and language of the tests are chosen at the top of the step: **equivalence partitioning** (as soon as an attribute has two partitions), **boundary value analysis** (ordered attributes only — 2 values by default: each limit and its neighbour outside the partition; 3 values on choice), **decision table testing** (as soon as a combined condition exists — **reduced** by default: one reference column, each input at its first valid value, then one column per other value of an input; the **full** table, every combination, is chosen under the techniques, with its number of columns: “Decision table: reduced (13 columns) · full (32 columns)”; two identical columns in two conditions make one case), **state transition testing** (as soon as a state exists: one case per transition), **error guessing** (input rules: text attributes that have an invalid partition; its card also offers **cross-cutting heuristics (outside the explicit rules)** — technical interruption, session expired, direct access without authentication, maximum length, double submission, browser back —, **unchecked** by default, each with its reason; one the requirement already talks about says « already covered by RB-10 » and cannot be checked; a checked heuristic adds one case, with no rule, filed under *Outside the explicit rules*, tagged `heuristic:<id>`, never counted in the rule coverage; when every blank input equals « empty field » — a rule removes the spaces — and that empty value is already tested by a finer technique, error guessing is **unchecked by default**, with the reason on its card, and can be checked again). A technique without any element is greyed with its reason and can be **forced** (then one case per rule) — never boundary values without an ordered attribute. A technique not allowed by the profile says **excluded by the profile**. A technique whose elements are all exercised by a finer technique you kept says **Covered by the decision table** (or **by the boundary values**) rather than “0 cases”. **There is no quantity to set**: the number of cases follows from the coverage elements, and a technique that produces nothing is not an error.

**6 · The plan, before any call.** The code combines the elements (**Each Choice**, one case per **expected behaviour**): the valid partitions become **one nominal case** whose rows each take other accepted values until every one is covered; the invalid partitions of the **same kind** (the same rules) become one case, **one row per partition and a single wrong value per row** — two kinds never mix; an input of a condition keeps its reference value outside the decision table, which alone combines it (country × postal code). A decision table follows the same principle: for a *Validation* condition, the accepted columns form **one** case, one row per column, and the refused columns one case **per kind**, a single wrong value per row; for a *Behaviour* condition, a column stays a case. A partition already exercised by boundary values or a decision table is not planned twice. The summary shows **« 12 planned cases · 8 / 8 rules covered »** before you generate — computed, not asked of the AI.

**7 · Generate — the AI proposes, it does not decide.** *Generate the cases* sends the corrected model and the plan (in batches of 20, **three at a time**, with the progress « Batch 1 / 2 written »; the cases of a batch show as soon as it is back, *Cancel* stops the batches not started yet). For each planned case the AI proposes the title, the precondition, concrete steps and observable expected results, citing the **parameters** as `@name`; it chooses neither the number, nor the values, nor the technique. A case without the rules it covers, without its coverage element, citing a parameter that has no data column, or never citing one of its parameters is refused and asked again once — so is an expected result that depends on a parameter (« if @country… »): every row shares the same expected results. The AI is told, row by row, which values vary: titles and expected results speak about them, not about the reference values. The result says **« 12 planned · 11 written »** when the AI did not write everything — a fact, never an error; a failed batch is reported with its member message and *Retry batch n*, and the others are kept. Once generated, the button becomes *Regenerate the N cases* and asks for confirmation — the cases, their corrections and their verdicts are replaced; if the plan changes afterwards, it says so (« The plan has changed: M cases (N generated) »). The report gives the **durations**: analysis, writing and each batch, in seconds.

**8 · Data-driven cases.** Every case has its **parameters** (`@country`, `@postalCode`) and a **data table**: one row per iteration — one run in Azure DevOps —, one column per parameter, filled from the representative values of the model. **Every row is complete**: every form input is a column, at its **reference value** (its first valid partition), and only the values the row exercises change — the tester never has to invent a value. On screen, reference values are **greyed**, varied values in plain text, the header of a column that varies is marked, and the legend says it once; spaces show as « ␣ » and an empty value as « (empty) ». Every parameter is cited in the steps. All rows of a case share the same expected results; an expected result that depends on the row is itself a parameter, or the case is split. In **Gherkin + examples**, the scenario cites the parameters and the data table **is** its examples, shown once.

**9 · Read, then edit in place.** The cases are **grouped**: **Nominal cases** first, then one group per **rule the case checks** (« RB-04 — Postal code valid for the country (2 cases) »), in the order of the rules — a valid case is never filed under a refusal rule. Every case opens for reading: the **precondition** first, then the numbered steps with the **parameters highlighted**, then « Data (N iterations) — add a row ». **Edit** switches to editing: title, priority, precondition, steps (add, remove, move with buttons) or the Gherkin scenario (checked as you type: Given, When, Then in that order), the **data table** cell by cell, rows and parameters added or removed. A case that cites a parameter without a column is **invalid** and says so; an incomplete row is flagged. An edited case keeps *Proposed by AI* with **edited**; *Restore the proposal* brings it back. **Add a case by hand**: choose its rule and its technique — no AI. *Remove the case* can be undone for 5 seconds.

**Checks — without AI.** Under the list of cases, the **findings** of the checks, computed again at every edit: a rule with no case, a partition no case exercises, a **duplicate** (same rule checked, same expected result, whatever the technique), contradicting expected results on the same row, an expected result that depends on a parameter, a parameter without a column or a column never cited, an incomplete row, two wrong values on one row, an input step citing no parameter, boundary values on an unordered input, a column that became infeasible, a blank input that equals « empty field » when a rule removes spaces. The partitions no case exercises are **named**, one line per rule; a case ignored or removed no longer counts, and *Complete the coverage…* then writes its planned case again. Each finding leads to its case; a duplicate or an overlap offers **Ignore this case** — the one of the least fine technique: the case stays visible, struck through, leaves the review, and *Restore* brings it back. **Complete the coverage…** is active only when a rule or a partition is missing: it writes only the planned cases missing from the list, sending those already there. In the summary, the **rule × technique matrix** counts each rule's cases per technique; a cell filters the list of cases, *Show all* brings it back.

**Review of the cases — the judge (AI), an opinion, never a decision.** On demand — **Check the cases** in the summary, with the number of cases not checked yet; it no longer starts by itself after a generation —, when *Designed-case review* is on (*Functions and data*, profile, and a **judge model** in *AI assistant › Connection*), a second model — never the one that proposed the cases — reads each case with the requirement and the test model, and gives a **verdict**: *holds*, *to rework* or *dropped*, with **issues** from a closed list: abstract step, unobservable expected result, missing data, data not representative, technique misapplied, coverage element not exercised, duplicate (with the case it duplicates), contradiction (with the other case), incompatible preconditions, not runnable by hand, conditional expected result, outside the requirement. Under each case, the checks come first (« Check »), then the judge's issues (« Judge (AI) »); an issue that cites another case links to it; for a case to rework, the proposed correction shows **before / after**, step by step or field by field (*Show the differences* for the word diff), with **Apply** (the case becomes edited, the verdict stays, « correction applied ») and **Ignore** (the issue is hidden). The judge never removes, ignores or writes a case, and it never touches the rules, the technique or the parameters. An edited case is *to review again*: *Review this case* reads it alone. The summary counts the verdicts, the frequent issues, the review model, its duration, and the issues or verdicts **discarded** by the product (a cited case that does not exist, a verdict without an issue). Without a judge model, or with the function turned off, the summary says why and everything else works.

**10 · Compared with the existing tests — locally, never by the AI.** Every case is compared at every edit with the test cases already linked to the requirement, first on **the rule checked and the expected result** (a linked test that carries the `covers:` tag of the rule and the same last expected result is identical, whatever its title), then by the significant words of their titles: **Identical**, **Similar** (named with a link) or **New**, with its default decision. The column on the right, at step 3, counts them and says what it compares on; no AI, no network call.

**Until you confirm, nothing is written.** The model, the cases and your changes live in the page: reloading clears them, and the browser warns you before you close the tab.

**11 · Review and write.** *Review and write →* opens a separate step (*Back to design* loses nothing): for each valid case, **Create**, **Update** (only when a matching test exists) or **Ignore**, pre-filled — and each pre-filled decision says where it comes from: *identical* or *close to an existing test* (the comparison), *proposed by the judge (AI): dropped* or *to rework*, with the issue, or *by default* —, with its consequence and a word-by-word diff; a case to rework shows its issues and *Apply the correction*; every decision can be changed; warnings before an update (already run, edited by hand recently, automated, in a plan). **Attach to a plan** is optional. *Write N cases…* opens a confirmation; only its button writes, with **your** rights, case by case. A created case gets its title, its steps — **the precondition as the first step without an expected result**, marked « Precondition: » —, its **parameters** and its **data** (one iteration per row, visible and runnable in the Test Runner), its priority, the requirement's area, the **Tested By** link, and the tags `niveau:<level>`, `type:<type>`, `technique:<technique>`, one `covers:<rule>` per rule and `ai-generated` (only for cases proposed by the AI; cases written before 0.2.6 keep their former origin tag, still recognised and shown, like the new one, by an **AI** pill on linked tests, never rewritten). An updated case changes only its title, steps, parameters, data and priority. The **report** lists each case — created, updated, ignored or refused — with the **iterations written**, the **generation model** and the **review model**, the number of cases **ignored on the judge's proposal**, *Retry attaching* when attaching failed, and a **CSV export with the data** when no case could be created. **Nothing is ever deleted** in Azure DevOps.

**When the AI is not available** — not set up, *Test design* turned off in the connection or by the project default, or access refused earlier in the page — the area still opens: the existing coverage is there, the test model can be built by hand and the plan is computed, *Analyse* and *Generate* are disabled with a sentence saying why, and a link to the AI assistant rules, in *My preferences › Project rules* (and, for a project administrator, to the administration).
