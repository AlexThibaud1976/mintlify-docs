**Where.** The same assessment shows in three places: step 0 **Requirement** of **Conception** (before the test model), the **Exigence** tab of a work item, and the **Requirement assessed** column of **Coverage**, which shows the verdict and score of the last saved assessment (“DoR ✓”, “DoR ✕”; the full label and the open non-critical questions in the tooltip) and opens it. Arriving in **Conception**, a requirement never assessed or whose assessment is outdated opens on step 0 **Requirement**; an up-to-date assessment opens the test model directly (step 1). A design in progress is never moved. In the step bar, the step 0 tile sums up the current assessment, read from the history without calling the assistant: the score and “DoR met” with the date and the revision read; “DoR not met” (its main reason is read by screen readers and written under **Why** in the header); “to reassess” when the story changed since; “not assessed”; “assessment not read” with **Try again** when reading fails; “assessment running…” during an assessment, then the result without changing step. Screen readers get the full verdict label; clicking the tile opens step 0.

**What happens.** Opening never calls the AI assistant: the saved assessment is read. **Assess the requirement** reads the story and its context (the same as **Context read**, below it), then the assistant scores each criterion of the profile's grid from 0 to 5 and cites its evidence — an identifier of the requirement (RB-, CA-…) or an exact quote. The product then checks every citation against the text that was sent, shows as **not verifiable** what depends on a source that was not read (links, code, development links), contests a gap whose words are on one line of the requirement, and flags a question of the requirement that was not taken up. Clicking a piece of evidence highlights it in **Context read** (tab **Code and context**).

**Score and verdict.** Both are **computed by the code** from the rules of the profile (section **Requirement assessment**): the score on the verifiable share of the grid, the verdict **Definition of Ready met**, **Definition of Ready not met** or **Definition of Ready undetermined** with every rule that fired. The assessment is about a story before development: it is the Definition of Ready, not the Definition of Done.

**Requirement questions.** The criticality of a question written in the requirement (Q-nn) is set by the code from what the requirement says about it: “blocking”, “critical”, “must be answered before…”, “high priority” make it critical; “optional”, “not blocking”, “nice to have”, “low priority” non-critical; a written answer (“Answer:”, ✓, struck-through text) closes it. With no such word, it is non-critical (rule set on the assessment bench). The priority proposed by the assistant stays shown, with “criticality set by the requirement” when it differs; the questions it adds from a finding keep its priority. Two assessments of the same requirement can therefore no longer flip because the assistant ranked a question of the requirement differently. A Definition of Ready met with questions still open says so — “1 open non-critical question: Q-06”, each question a link to its row — on screen, in the PDF and in the Coverage tooltip. **Narrowly** is added when the score is within the profile's margin of the threshold (5 points by default, 0 turns it off) and is the only rule in play.

**Gestures.** Close a question (answered or off topic) — **Undo** reopens it —, correct the nature of a finding, mark a criterion not applicable: score and verdict are recomputed **without calling the assistant**, and each gesture is dated and signed in the saved assessment. When the work item has changed since, the assessment is marked **outdated**; **Reassess** runs it again. When an improvement is written to the story, the assessment it came from also records it among its gestures — “Improvement written to the story”, with the written revision and the criteria added, amended or removed — and in its PDF; score and verdict do not change.

**Reassessing an unchanged requirement.** The score can vary by several points from one assessment to the next while the story has not changed (model variability); near the threshold, the verdict can flip. So when the revision of the work item is the one of the last saved assessment, **Reassess** — in the header or in the **narrowly** tag — first opens a warning: the revision and date of that assessment, the risks (the score may move, the verdict may flip — with the current margin when it is narrow —, a new call is billed to the project’s AI connection, the current assessment stays in the history and the new one becomes the current one) and the advice: improve the requirement, then reassess it. **Cancel** (selected by default, or Escape) calls nothing; **Reassess anyway** starts the reassessment as before. Nothing changes in the computation, the number of calls or the thresholds. A first assessment, or a story whose revision has changed, is assessed without a warning.

**History.** Every assessment is kept, even when the story has not changed — 20 by default, from 5 to 100 in the profile's **Requirement assessment** section; beyond that, the oldest is removed. The **History** tab (also **History (n)** in the “…” menu) lists the date, revision, score, verdict, model and author, then the gestures, with the score curve against the Definition of Ready threshold. An entry opens read-only (“Assessment of &lt;date&gt;, revision &lt;n&gt;”); **Back to the current assessment** returns to the current one. Reading the history never calls the assistant. If the history cannot be read (storage unavailable, unreadable document), the screen says so — “The history of this work item could not be read.” — with **Try again**, instead of showing the requirement as never assessed.

**One decision, one screen.** On top of the page, the requirement is named once (“#id · title”, then type, state, revision and **Change requirement**); **Existing coverage** fits on one line (“N linked tests · per technique · matches”), folded on first display — assessed or not — and remembered for you and this requirement. **Not assessed yet**, the requirement has the same anatomy: the header “Requirement assessment · not assessed yet”, a help sentence and **Assess the requirement**; the **What will be sent** line (“Requirement ≈ N tokens · L links · P attachments · code: F files read (≈ T tokens)”, or “code: not read” with **Read the code**), each item opening the detail; then a single **Code and context** panel, folded and remembered, holding **Linked code**, **Context read** and **What is sent to the model** — the only place this table appears at step 0. The **Exigence** tab of a work item has the same anatomy. The header is the only block always shown: the score (“56,9 / 100” in French), the verdict, the threshold, one filled button that follows the state — **Assess the requirement**, **Reassess revision N** when the story changed, **Improve the requirement** when the DoR is not met, **Continue to the model →** in Conception when it is met; **Reassess** and the “…” menu (History, PDF, Improve) as secondary — then a **Why** line: the rules that fired, **one per cause** (“2 critical questions open”, “No acceptance criteria”, “Estimable ★ 2 / 5, minimum 3”, “Small ★ 2 / 5, minimum 3”, “Score 56.9 below the threshold 75”), each a button that opens the tab that deals with it, and a help line (date, model, verdict computed by the rules, share of the grid scored and why — for instance no link or no code read). Below, **tabs with a single panel open** (keyboard arrows, Home, End): **Questions for the PO** (critical first, *Answered* / *Off topic* / *Undo*, *Copy the open questions*, origin in plain words), **Criteria** (*Below target* by default with a one-line summary of the criteria at target and of those not assessable, with **Read the code**; *All* grouped by family; **Show the families** for the radar), **Findings** (gaps then deductions, with the question they led to; facts folded; **Correct the nature** on demand), **Split**, **Code and context** (linked code, context read, **What goes to the model** — its only place —, technical details) and **History**. The tab open by default: Questions if a critical question is still open, otherwise Criteria if a criterion is below target, otherwise the last tab you chose for this requirement. No internal identifier (q2, f7, estimable=2, “na”) shows outside **Technical details**; the work item tab **Exigence**, step 0’s tile (“Requirement · 56,9 · DoR not met”), the PDF and the settings preview use the same wording.

**Bars and targets.** Each criterion has a **target**: 3/5, or 4/5 for an essential criterion (★) — shown in the **Target** column of the criteria in the administration. The bar shows the score against the target, its colour follows the gap (at the target, above, one below, two or more below) and **the gap is always written** (“−1 below the target”, “= at the target”, “+1 above”). The target is for reading only: the score and the DoR verdict do not change. **Effective weight**: a criterion weighs its family’s weight divided by the active criteria of the family (by default INVEST 7.5 points, Definition 5, Criteria 6.7, Links and Flow 3.3) — shown in the administration, in each row’s details and on the bar; a criterion turned off passes its weight to the others of its family. A family whose criteria are all turned off by the administrator is taken out of the total: the score and the verifiable share are on the other families. The grid reads Criterion · Score and target · Score · Gap · Why (first sentence); the evidence and *Not applicable* are in each row’s details; a criterion that is not assessable reads “not counted”.

**PDF export.** **Export to PDF**, from the current assessment or from a history entry, downloads `Evaluation-US-<id>-rev<n>-<date>.pdf`: the score, the verifiable share, the verdict and its rules, the family radar, the **scores and targets** (the same bars as on screen, gap written), the criteria table, the code checks, the questions with their priority, audience, source and state, the split, the three next actions, the sources read and not read with their reason, the gestures, and an **Evolution** appendix. It never contains the connection key, the resource address or the text sent to the assistant.

**What it does not do.** Nothing is written to the story; the proposed split creates no work item.

**Administration.** Turn on **Requirement assessment** in **AI assistant › Functions and data**, then set the Definition of Ready, the weights and the criteria for the whole project in **Requirements and design**, where a live preview shows their effect on the project’s latest assessments; in **My preferences › Project rules**, a member reads this project setting, whatever the plan chosen. Configuration measured on the bench: gpt-6-sol without reasoning, with an answer limit of at least 12,000 tokens; below that, the assessment of a long requirement may be cut, and the screen says so with the limit.

**Designing on an unmet DoR.** The profile setting **Designing a requirement whose DoR is not met** (section **Requirement assessment**) decides what **Conception** does when the up-to-date assessment of the requirement does not meet the Definition of Ready. **Allow**: nothing more than the step 0 tile. **Warn** (recommended, default): at the first action that starts the design (**Analyse the requirement**, entering the model by hand), a dialog recalls the revision, the date and the main reason of the verdict; **Back to the requirement** or **Design anyway** — that choice is remembered for you, this work item and this revision. **Block**: steps 1 to 3 stay visible but inactive; a panel offers **Improve the requirement** and **Reassess**, and an administrator can **Design anyway (administrator)** for the page. A design already started is never locked, and step 0, the history, the PDF and the cases already designed stay available. The verdict remains advice; when the assessment function is off, the setting has no effect.
